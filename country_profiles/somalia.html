Aviation System Overview

Somalia's aviation system operates in one of the world's most challenging security environments. Mogadishu Aden Adde International Airport (HCMM) is the primary gateway, located at 9m ASL on the Indian Ocean coast. The airport operates under a layered security framework established by AMISOM (now ATMIS — African Union Transition Mission in Somalia) and Somali security forces. Despite the security environment, HCMM handles a significant volume of international humanitarian, UN, diplomatic, and commercial traffic.

The airport perimeter has been the target of multiple Al-Shabaab attacks including vehicle-borne IED attempts and mortar fire. The main terminal building has been rebuilt following the 2010 suicide bombing. HCMM operates under strict security protocols: all movements airside require security escort; no unaccompanied walking on the apron; crew must remain in the aircraft or in designated secure facilities when on the ground.

Electronic warfare interference on approach to HCMM has been reported by multiple operators. GNSS spoofing or jamming affecting approach accuracy has been documented in PIREPs, particularly during periods of heightened conflict activity. Operators should not rely solely on GNSS for approaches and should maintain ILS/VOR backup capability.

Jubba Airways provides the main commercial ground handling services at HCMM. The Civil Aviation Authority of Somalia (CAAS) is the regulatory body but has extremely limited technical capacity. All carriers serving Mogadishu are subject to EU Air Safety List restrictions or enhanced oversight requirements. Night operations are strongly discouraged due to the combined security and approach risk profile.

Beyond Mogadishu, aviation in Somalia is fragmented. Hargeisa (HCMH) in Somaliland is a comparatively stable alternative, functioning under separate Somaliland administration. Garowe and Bosaso serve Puntland. These airports carry their own security and infrastructure risk profiles distinct from the Mogadishu environment.

Top-Line Security Assessment

Somalia carries a HIGH overall risk rating for aviation operations, with the Mogadishu airport environment rated as EXTREME for ground-level security exposure. Al-Shabaab (Harakat al-Shabaab al-Mujahideen) is the primary threat actor and has demonstrated a persistent intent to target aviation assets, airport infrastructure, and international actors associated with the Somali federal government.

Al-Shabaab has documented MANPADS capability, acquired during the collapse of the Somali state in the early 1990s from abandoned military stocks. While no confirmed MANPADS shoot-down of a commercial aircraft has occurred, the threat is credible and was explicitly cited by ICAO in a 2016 airspace risk assessment. All operators should apply war-risk MANPADS loading for HCMM operations.

The airport has been attacked multiple times, most significantly in August 2010 (suicide bomb targeting AMISOM/Uganda contingent) and multiple mortar attacks on the airport perimeter and apron area. The ATMIS (formerly AMISOM) military presence provides a security envelope but this is not equivalent to a conflict-free environment. Airport security is layered and intensive but cannot fully eliminate the risk from IEDs, indirect fire, or armed assault.

GNSS interference on approach to HCMM has been reported by multiple operators and is documented in ICAO safety circulars. The interference may be deliberate (electronic warfare by Al-Shabaab or sympathetic actors) or incidental from military jamming systems. In either case, operators must not rely solely on GNSS for approach procedures. ILS or VOR/DME backup must be maintained.

Outside Mogadishu, Al-Shabaab controls significant rural territory and has the capability to interdict ground movements and airstrip access in southern and central Somalia. No commercial aviation operations should be conducted to secondary strips in Al-Shabaab-influenced areas without ATMIS/SNA security coordination.

Active Threat Vectors  [2025–2026]
HIGH Al-Shabaab  ·  MANPADS capability & airport targeting

Al-Shabaab has documented MANPADS (Man-Portable Air Defence Systems) capability and has explicitly threatened aviation targets. Previous attacks on HCMM include mortar fire on apron areas, VBIED attempts at airport access, and direct assault on terminal building. The group maintains a dedicated intelligence collection capability against HCMM operations including monitoring of scheduled movements.

ICAO issued a MANPADS threat advisory for HCMM in 2016. The advisory remains relevant. All departures and approaches should be conducted using MANPADS risk mitigation procedures where available (steep approaches, tactical departure procedures, reduced exposure time at low altitude over populated areas).

Aviation exposure: MANPADS threat on departure/approach; mortar/indirect fire risk on apron; VBIED risk on access roads; assault risk at perimeter. War risk insurance mandatory.

HIGH GNSS interference  ·  Approach integrity degraded

Multiple PIREPs report GNSS signal degradation, spoofing, and jamming on approach to HCMM. Incidents have caused false position indications and navigation uncertainty below FL100. The phenomenon is documented in ICAO safety circulars and NOTAM advisories. Sources may include military EW systems, deliberate Al-Shabaab jamming, or opportunistic interference from the complex radio environment in Mogadishu.

Aviation exposure: do not rely solely on GNSS for approaches to HCMM. Maintain ILS/VOR capability. Monitor GPS integrity warnings on approach. Cross-check position with ILS glidepath and DME. RAIM prediction not reliable for planning at HCMM.

HIGH Night operations  ·  Compounded security & approach risk

Night operations at HCMM carry compounded risk from two independent sources: the security threat (Al-Shabaab has conducted attacks at night and under cover of darkness, and ATMIS/SNA situational awareness is reduced after dark); and the approach risk (GNSS interference combined with reduced visual cues creates elevated CFIT risk at night). Multiple operators have adopted a formal no-night-ops policy at HCMM.

Aviation exposure: Night operations at HCMM should be avoided unless operationally essential and only with current security SITREP and ILS approaches confirmed serviceable. Briefing on night security situation from ATMIS mandatory before any night landing.

MODERATE Ground transit  ·  IED & ambush risk — Mogadishu city

Mogadishu city transit carries extreme risk. Al-Shabaab conducts IED attacks, VBIED attacks, and targeted assassinations in the city, including against hotel complexes used by international operators and NGO staff. Transit outside the airport compound requires armoured vehicles and armed escort at all times.

Aviation exposure: crew hotel accommodation and transit in Mogadishu requires armoured vehicle, armed escort, and pre-movement route reconnaissance. No unaccompanied movements. Minimise time outside airport compound.

Seasonal Calendar — Somalia Bimodal Rainfall
Apr – Jun DERA
Dera (Long rains) · Primary season
Somalia's main rainy season. Mogadishu receives significant rainfall with CB activity, gusty surface winds, and reduced visibility periods. HCMM approach sector affected by low-level convection from the Indian Ocean. Runway surface water and crosswinds on RWY 05/23. Inland areas of southern Somalia receive heavier precipitation with flash flooding possible.
Jul – Sep
Hagaa (Cool dry) · Best operational window
Indian Ocean monsoon brings dry, cooler conditions to coastal Somalia. Mogadishu experiences its most stable meteorological conditions. Best visibility, lowest convective activity. Strong southwest monsoon winds can create significant crosswind on RWY 05/23, particularly July. Best operational weather window for the year.
Oct – Nov
Deyr (Short rains) · Secondary season
Second rainy period. Generally less intense than Dera but still significant. CB activity possible over coastal areas. Approach sector wind variability increases. Flash flooding in low-lying areas around HCMM perimeter possible during peak events.
Dec – Mar
Jilaal (Cool dry) · Secondary stable period
Cool and dry. Northeast monsoon. Generally stable approach conditions at Mogadishu. Low convective activity. Some harmattan dust possible in northern Somalia from continental airflow. Good operational conditions overall with security threat remaining constant irrespective of season.
