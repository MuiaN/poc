Aviation System Overview

Kenya operates one of the most developed and commercially active aviation systems in sub-Saharan Africa. The country's infrastructure is anchored by Jomo Kenyatta International Airport in Nairobi, a Category 9 RFFS hub handling over five million passengers annually and serving as the primary gateway for East Africa across 51 international destinations. JKIA operates two instrument runways with full IFR capability, Class C/D controlled airspace, and a full ATC environment including approach radar. It is the hub of Kenya Airways and functions as a critical transfer node for regional and intercontinental traffic.

Moi International Airport in Mombasa is Kenya's second international gateway, serving the coastal tourism economy and designated as the primary diversion alternate for JKIA. It handles a significant volume of European leisure charter traffic during the November-to-March peak season, alongside scheduled regional and domestic services. Moi's operational envelope is shaped by coastal weather variability, particularly crosswinds during the October-December short rains and sea fog during morning operations.

Wilson Airport in Nairobi is one of the busiest general aviation facilities in Africa, supporting a diverse mix of domestic charter, humanitarian, safari, and training operations. Its proximity to Nairobi National Park and shared terminal airspace with JKIA create a complex and high-density operational environment. Wilson is the origin point for most light aircraft and turboprop operations serving the Maasai Mara, northern Kenya, and cross-border humanitarian routes into South Sudan and Somalia.

Beyond the three main hubs, Kenya maintains a dense network of domestic airstrips — over 400 registered landing surfaces — concentrated in the tourism and humanitarian sectors. The Maasai Mara circuit alone has eight active commercial airstrips. These semi-prepared surfaces are the primary source of operational risk in the Kenya aviation system, driven by wildlife incursion, wet and soft ground during the long rains, and limited emergency response capability. The risk profile at major international airports is low to moderate and well managed; the elevated risk profile belongs firmly in the light aircraft and last-mile environment.

Top-Line Security Assessment

Kenya's security environment is broadly stable but operationally sensitive. The threat landscape is defined not by systemic instability or directed targeting of aviation assets, but by a combination of geographically contained extremist activity, climate-driven infrastructure stress, and persistent urban criminality that collectively elevate ground-level exposure for operators and travellers.

Al-Shabaab remains the primary structured threat; active along the north-eastern and coastal border corridors — Mandera, Garissa, Wajir, and Lamu — where IED activity against security personnel and ground movements is documented and recurring. This threat is real but bounded; it does not meaningfully affect operations at Nairobi or Mombasa.

Urban environments present a different but consistent risk profile. Opportunistic crime, traffic-related vulnerability, and last-mile exposure are the dominant concerns for crew and passenger ground movements, particularly in Nairobi and Mombasa during peak congestion and the long rains season.

The 2027 electoral cycle is beginning to register at the community level and warrants forward monitoring, given Kenya's history of election-period disruption to transport and public movement.

The primary aviation exposure across all threat categories remains the ground environment — not the airfield. Airside operations at HKJK and HKMO are stable, well-regulated, and low-risk.

Active Threat Vectors  [Jan–Apr 2026]
HIGH Al-Shabaab  ·  Northern & coastal border corridors

Mandera, Garissa, Wajir, and Lamu. Persistent, geographically contained. IED attack 16 Jan 2026 — 2 KIA (police reservists), 2 WIA. Lamu/Boni Dodori corridor monitored due to historical militant movement and Somalia border proximity.

Aviation exposure: ground transit on access routes to northern airstrips (Wajir, Mandera, Lokichoggio); security of remote aprons; risk to ground personnel and visiting crew at border-proximate airstrips.

MODERATE Climate-driven infrastructure stress  ·  Long rains disruption

Heavy rains and flash flooding Mar–Apr 2026. Nairobi flash flooding 6–7 Mar — fatalities, stranded motorists, JKIA flight delays and diversions, access road failures. Infrastructure fragility — terminal power inconsistencies, drainage failures, landside mobility degraded.

Aviation exposure: ground access to JKIA; diversion traffic spike to HKMO; soft ground at Maasai Mara and northern strips; airstrip surface integrity at semi-prepared locations.

MODERATE Urban opportunistic crime  ·  Nairobi & Mombasa

Smash-and-grab, distraction theft, and movement delays along key transit corridors. Makupa Causeway (HKMO access), Airport Road and Mombasa Road (HKJK), Kisauni/Nyali/Likoni corridors elevated. Panga Boys gang activity resurging in Mombasa peri-urban zones. Cargo theft at airside periphery and fuel diversion reported.

Aviation exposure: crew and passenger ground transit (particularly after dark); cargo at airside periphery; fuel integrity at remote airstrips.

WATCH 2027 Electoral cycle  ·  Early mobilisation

Periodic rallies and public gatherings observed. No sustained unrest during reporting period. Historical precedent of aviation and transport disruption during Kenyan electoral cycles.

Aviation exposure: potential for KAA/KCAA labour action; access disruption to HKJK during mobilisation events; elevated risk of politically driven public assembly near transport nodes. Monitor as 2027 cycle matures.

Density altitude calculations straightforward. Lowest weather-related hull and liability exposure across the calendar year.
