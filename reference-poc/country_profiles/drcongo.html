Aviation System Overview

The Democratic Republic of Congo operates one of Africa's most complex and highest-risk aviation systems. Its two primary international airports present starkly different risk profiles: N'djili International Airport (FZAA) in Kinshasa serves as the western hub in a moderate-risk but infrastructure-constrained environment, while Goma International Airport (FZNA) in North Kivu operates in an active conflict zone and must be treated as a war-risk operation in every respect.

N'djili International (FZAA) at 313m ASL is the country's primary gateway, serving most scheduled international traffic. Ground handling is provided by FILAIR. Infrastructure is aging and RFFS capability, while nominally adequate, is limited in surge capacity. The airport's position in Kinshasa — a megacity of 15+ million — creates significant ground transit risk and logistics complexity. Fuel is available but supply chain reliability is below regional norms; operators should confirm availability and carry contingency fuel plans.

Goma International Airport (FZNA) at 1,490m ASL is situated in one of the world's most complex conflict environments. M23 armed group, backed by Rwandan support, controls significant territory around Goma including periods of airport access control. The airport is also geologically exposed — it sits adjacent to the Nyiragongo volcano, whose 2021 eruption deposited lava on the runway perimeter and disrupted operations for an extended period. Armed escort for all ground movements is mandatory. Night operations are strongly discouraged. The risk rating at FZNA is extreme.

DRC's Civil Aviation Authority (Autorité de l'Aviation Civile — AAC) has been on the EU Air Safety List since 2015, with all Congolese-registered carriers banned from EU airspace. The regulatory environment is severely limited and operators cannot rely on ICAO-standard oversight being applied to ground service providers, maintenance facilities, or ATC services at secondary airports.

Top-Line Security Assessment

DR Congo carries a HIGH overall risk rating — one of the most complex and dangerous operating environments in Africa for aviation. The country is effectively split between two distinct risk environments: the west (Kinshasa basin, Katanga) which, while challenging, permits managed operations; and the east (North/South Kivu, Ituri, Tanganyika province) which must be treated as an active conflict zone with war-risk loading applied to every operation.

Eastern DRC has experienced continuous armed conflict for over three decades. As of 2025–2026, the dominant threat at Goma (FZNA) is M23, an armed group with documented Rwandan military support that controls territory immediately surrounding the airport. FZNA has been subject to ground fighting, access restriction, and checkpoint control by armed groups. All FZNA operations require armed escort for all ground movements without exception.

The Allied Democratic Forces (ADF) operate in the Beni/Butembo axis in North Kivu and into Ituri province, conducting targeted civilian attacks. The Forces Democratiques de Liberation du Rwanda (FDLR) — a Rwandan Hutu militia — operates in South Kivu. Multiple other armed groups (CODECO, FPIC, various Mai-Mai factions) control territory across Ituri and the Kivus.

Kinshasa (FZAA) is a different risk category: stable enough for managed international operations but with significant ground-level security risks including crime, political protest, and logistics fragility. The city's size and infrastructure limitations create substantial ground transit risk for crew and passengers.

The Nyiragongo volcano adjacent to Goma presents an additional non-conflict hazard: its 2021 eruption deposited lava to the airport perimeter, disrupted operations for months, and may repeat. Monitor volcanic activity NOTAMs for ash advisory and lava flow proximity at FZNA.

Active Threat Vectors  [2025–2026]
HIGH M23 armed conflict  ·  Goma / FZNA area — EXTREME

M23 (Mouvement du 23 Mars), backed by Rwanda, controls territory surrounding Goma including access roads to FZNA. The group has occupied parts of Goma city and airport access has been subject to armed checkpoint control. As of 2025, M23 and FARDC (DRC army) positions change regularly — ground security must be assessed in real time before every operation.

Armed escort by FARDC, MONUSCO, or vetted private security is mandatory for all ground movements at FZNA. Do not transit the perimeter or access roads without escort. Night operations strongly discouraged — curfew conditions variable.

Aviation exposure: EXTREME. Every FZNA operation requires: current security assessment; armed escort; pre-arranged departure clearance; confirmed fuel (unreliable supply); and contingency planning for immediate extraction if security situation deteriorates.

HIGH ADF / Ituri militia  ·  North Kivu & Ituri province

Allied Democratic Forces conduct regular attacks on civilians in North Kivu (Beni sector) and Ituri province. CODECO and FPIC militias control territory in Ituri. Bunia airport (FZKE) is MONUSCO-supported but surrounding areas are active conflict zone. No non-escorted operations to Bunia or secondary Ituri strips.

Aviation exposure: Bunia and all secondary Ituri/North Kivu strips — armed escort mandatory; MONUSCO coordination recommended; no cargo operations without security clearance; no night operations.

MODERATE Nyiragongo volcano  ·  FZNA ash & lava risk

Nyiragongo volcano, one of the world's most active, is located 20km north of Goma. The May 2021 eruption sent lava flows to the airport perimeter, forced temporary closure, and disrupted fuel supply infrastructure for months. Current eruption risk is LOW per VONA monitoring but the hazard is persistent and can escalate rapidly.

Aviation exposure: monitor VONA (Volcano Observatory Notice for Aviation) and ash NOTAMs before FZNA operations; lava flow risk to taxiways and perimeter; ash plume can extend to FL200+; crater lake overturning risk exists.

MODERATE Kinshasa ground  ·  Crime & logistics fragility (FZAA)

Kinshasa presents moderate ground-level risk: vehicle crime, opportunistic robbery, political protest risk, and significant traffic congestion create a demanding ground environment. Fuel supply at FZAA is generally available but confirmation 48hrs prior is essential — supply chain disruptions occur. FILAIR ground handling is functional but at a lower standard than East African comparators.

Aviation exposure: vetted ground transport mandatory; confirm fuel and permits well in advance; no unaccompanied crew movements in Kinshasa. Airside environment at FZAA is moderate risk, ground side is elevated risk.

Seasonal Calendar — DRC Equatorial & Highland Rainfall
Oct – May LONG WET
Long wet season · Kinshasa basin & eastern highlands
DRC straddles the equator and is one of the world's wettest countries. The Congo basin receives near-continuous precipitation. Kinshasa (FZAA) experiences heavy rain Oct–May with intense afternoon CB. Eastern highland areas (FZNA, FZMA) receive heavy orographic rainfall driven by the Albertine Rift. CB tops exceeding FL500 common. Operations at both airports affected by convective delays.
Jun – Sep
Dry season · Best operational window
June to September represents a relative dry period across most of DRC, particularly the southern and central regions. Kinshasa experiences its driest period. Reduced convective activity. Best visibility conditions. Eastern highlands still receive some rain — Goma (FZNA) has less seasonal variation due to equatorial position and lake influence. Overall best operational weather window.
Year-round
Equatorial Congo basin — year-round convection
The Congo equatorial basin generates some of the most intense convective activity on earth. Lightning strike frequency is the highest globally. FZAA is subject to year-round afternoon CB activity even during the nominal dry season. CB systems can develop extremely rapidly and produce severe turbulence, hail, and sudden windshifts. SIGMETs are frequent and should always be checked.
